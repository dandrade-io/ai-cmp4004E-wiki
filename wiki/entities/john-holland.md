# John H. Holland

Proposed **genetic algorithms** as a general model that could adapt to any process (slide 2 of [[slides-04-optimization]]). Per his 1992 article he developed the GA "by the mid-1960s" and then the **classifier system** (rules encoded as bit strings) to represent arbitrary programs ([[paper-holland-genetic-algorithms]], PDF p.2). His book *Adaptation in Natural and Artificial Systems* is dated 1975 (Univ. of Michigan Press, as cited in [[paper-dorigo-ant-system-1996]]) with a 1992 MIT Press edition (cited in the 1992 article's reading list and in [[paper-particle-swarm-optimization]]).

Bio (from the article, p.2): professor of psychology and of EECS at the University of Michigan; B.S. physics MIT 1950; worked on IBM's first programmed electronic computer (701) 1950–52; Santa Fe Institute steering committee since 1987.

> ⚠️ Contradiction (resolved): slide 5 labels him "1992" and slide 2 says 1975. The raw paper is a 1992 *Scientific American* article; 1975 is the original book. Neither is the "invention" date (mid-1960s).

Related: [[evolutionary-computation]], [[lawrence-fogel]], [[crossover-and-mutation]].

## In Eiben & Smith
Credited with the genetic algorithm (*Adaptation in Natural and Artificial Systems*), initially a means of studying adaptive behaviour; the Michigan-style [[learning-classifier-systems]] were first described by Holland in 1976 (pp.99, 108). Uniform/one-point crossover: one-point was "the original recombination operator proposed" by Holland (p.52).
